Water Damage to Hardwood Floors in Flagstaff, AZ

Home / Blog / Saving Water-Damaged Hardwood Floors in Flagstaff, AZ

A hardwood floor adds real value to a home, yet water can damage it more readily than almost any other feature. Cupping, crowning, buckling, staining, and mold in the subfloor beneath are all results of water reaching hardwood. For Flagstaff, AZ homeowners with hardwood floors, it is important to understand how to respond to water exposure and when professional restoration can save your floors versus when replacement is necessary.

What Water Does to Wood Floors

Because wood is hygroscopic, it takes in moisture and gives it back as the conditions around it change. Wood fibers expand as they take on moisture, which is what happens when a floor gets too wet. This causes cupping (edges rising higher than the center of boards), which is the first visible sign of moisture imbalance. With continued exposure, a board can buckle, lifting completely off the subfloor, or crown, its middle higher than the edges because the upper face dries quicker than the underside. When a floor stays wet a long time, mold can also develop under the boards, on the subfloor, unnoticed until they are pulled.

Will Your Wood Floors Survive Water Damage?

Five things decide if the floor is salvageable: the speed of the professional drying response, how much water there was, the time the boards spent wet, what kind of hardwood and finish you have, and what shape the subfloor is in. Floors exposed to clean water can often be saved when professional drying starts within 24 to 48 hours. What makes it work is slow, controlled drying, so moisture leaves the wood evenly and the boards do not split or crack.

Drying Wood Floors the Professional Way

Wood floors need a specialized approach that differs from the standard methods used on the rest of a structure. Technicians lay panels or drying mats on top of the floor, which sets up controlled conditions and draws moisture out of the boards evenly. Another option is to run a floor drying system under the subfloor, reaching it through the crawl space. Everything depends on an even, gradual pace, since wood dried too fast loses surface moisture before the core does and ends up permanently cupped, cracked, and split.

When Replacement Is Necessary

As a rule, hardwood has to be replaced after more than 48 to 72 hours of being wet, after contact with contaminated water such as floodwater or sewage, when delamination or buckling is widespread, when mold is growing on the subfloor, or when the subfloor has itself been damaged.
